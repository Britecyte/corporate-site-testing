Fat facts

Let's get the fat facts.

Big questions. Real answers. No beating around the F word.

Fat deserves better PR. And we're here to set the record straight.

From what Lipoderma actually is to where the fat comes from and what to expect, we've got answers to the questions worth asking.

1. Let's talk fat.

Turns out, there's more to fat than losing it.

Lipoderma. What's all the fat fuss about?

Lipoderma is a human adipose tissue allograft designed to help restore lost fat in areas where it naturally exists. Think fat replacing fat, helping bring back the softness, fullness, and contours that can change with age or weight loss.

Wait, fat is actually a good thing?

Turns out, fat deserves a little more credit.

The fat beneath your skin plays a key structural role, providing the support, softness, and fullness that contribute to a youthful appearance. And while collagen helps maintain skin firmness and elasticity, it can't replace the volume and structural support that fat provides.

As we age or lose weight, those fat pads can diminish, contributing to hollowing, sagging, and changes in facial contours.

Before you fill, let's talk fat. How is Lipoderma different from fillers?

Traditional dermal fillers, such as hyaluronic acid fillers, use gel-based materials to add volume. Lipoderma also restores volume, but takes a different approach by using real human adipose tissue to replace fat where it's been lost.

It's fat replacing fat, a like-for-like approach that restores the natural softness, fullness, and structural support of adipose tissue beneath the skin. Over time, the implanted tissue gradually remodels as your body replaces it with its own tissue, supporting natural-looking rejuvenation.

Where could I use a little more fat?

Fat looks good in more places than you might think.

Lipoderma is used for targeted, smaller-volume restoration, particularly in areas of the face, neck, and hands where fat loss has affected natural fullness and contours.

Your provider can help determine which areas may benefit.

Do I need liposuction first?

Nope. That's part of the beauty of it.

Unlike traditional fat grafting, which requires harvesting fat from your own body, Lipoderma uses donated human adipose tissue that's ready for your provider to prepare and implant. No liposuction harvest required.

Lost the weight. Miss the fat?

You worked hard for your weight loss. That doesn't mean you have to love every change that comes with it.

Weight loss, including weight loss associated with GLP-1 medications, can reduce facial fat and change your natural contours. Lipoderma may be an option for restoring fullness in those areas.

Your provider can help create a personalized restoration plan that fits your weight loss journey and evolves with your results.

2. Fat needs a glow-up era, too.

Every glow-up has a story. This one starts with a change you can see and evolves with you.

When does my glow-up begin?

You'll notice a change right after implantation, but that's just the beginning.

Over the following months, your body gradually remodels the implanted tissue, replacing it with your own tissue. This process takes time, and your results will continue to evolve along the way.

And the glow-up doesn't stop there. By restoring the underlying fat layer, Lipoderma helps reestablish the structural support that contributes to natural facial contours, a smoother-looking appearance, and healthy-looking skin.

Will my fat pull a disappearing act?

Maybe a little, but don't worry. It's all part of the process.

As your body gradually breaks down the implanted tissue to make way for your own tissue, you may notice a temporary decrease in fullness. Not everyone experiences this phase, and the degree of change can vary.

Think of it as part of your body's natural remodeling process, as the implanted tissue makes way for tissue of your own.

Will I need a touch-up?

Sometimes a little more fat is part of the plan.

Because your results continue to evolve during the remodeling process, we recommend checking in with your provider around 3–6 months to assess your progress and determine whether additional volume could help achieve your desired results.

Not everyone will need a touch-up. It's all about creating a personalized restoration plan that evolves with you.

How long does Lipoderma last? Longer than my last situationship?

We certainly hope so.

We expect results to last at least 12 months, with longer-term outcomes still being monitored. Because Lipoderma undergoes a gradual remodeling process, results can vary from person to person.

And whatever you do, don't go looking for a rebound in the first few months. Your body is busy doing its thing, and your results are still evolving. Patience looks good on you.

What's the downtime?

Good news. Life doesn't have to go on pause.

Most patients can get back to their everyday routines pretty quickly. Some temporary swelling, bruising, or tenderness may occur, but downtime is generally minimal.

Your provider will walk you through what to expect and any short-term precautions, so you can get back to doing your thing.

3. Whose fat? Glad you asked.

Let's talk about where the fat comes from, what makes it different, and why that matters.

Wait, whose fat are we talking about?

Not yours. And that's part of the beauty of it.

Lipoderma is made from donated human adipose tissue. The medical term is allograft, which simply means tissue donated by one person for use in another.

And when it comes to donated tissue, safety is a top priority. All donor screening, eligibility determination, and infectious-disease testing are performed in accordance with applicable FDA regulations and tissue banking standards. The tissue then undergoes specialized processing before being made available for implantation.

All the benefits of a fat-based approach, without having to harvest fat from your own body.

But like… doesn't that give you the ick?

We get it. Donated fat might sound a little unusual at first. But hear us out.

If you needed a heart, liver, or even a bone graft, would you think twice about accepting donated human tissue? Fat deserves the same consideration.

And here's a fun fat fact: adipose tissue is one of the body's largest endocrine organs, playing an important role in hormone signaling, energy storage, and much more. It also provides the structural support that helps maintain youthful-looking contours.

Turns out, fat is pretty incredible. Maybe it's time we gave it a little more credit.

What makes this fat so special?

Mother Nature knew what she was doing. And who are we to argue?

Lipoderma undergoes Britecyte's proprietary processing method, designed to remove components associated with an immune response while preserving the tissue's native architecture.

That means retaining both the fat cells (adipocytes) and the supportive framework surrounding them, known as the extracellular matrix.

Lipoderma is the only off-the-shelf human adipose allograft designed to preserve this complete native architecture, closely resembling the structure of natural fat.

Because Mother Nature designed fat that way for a reason.

Wait, are we talking about living fat cells or stem cells?

Nope. Let's clear that up.

Lipoderma is a devitalized human adipose tissue allograft. That means the fat cells are no longer viable, but the tissue's natural architecture, including the adipocytes and surrounding extracellular matrix, remains intact.

And no, this isn't a stem-cell treatment. Lipoderma is designed for like-for-like restoration, helping repair, replace, or reconstruct adipose tissue where it's been lost.

Okay, but is donated fat safe?

We're glad you asked. Safety is kind of a big deal.

Lipoderma is made from donated human tissue that undergoes donor screening, infectious-disease testing, and specialized processing in accordance with applicable FDA regulations and tissue banking standards. The tissue is processed through FDA-registered tissue establishments.

As with any procedure involving implanted human tissue, there are potential risks, including infection, inflammatory reactions, and other complications. Your provider will review these risks with you and help determine whether Lipoderma is appropriate for you.

Is Lipoderma FDA-approved?

Let's get the terminology right.

Lipoderma is FDA-regulated, not FDA-approved. And that's okay. Here's why.

Lipoderma falls under a category of human tissue products (HCT/Ps) that, when they meet specific FDA criteria, do not require premarket approval like drugs or certain medical devices.

Instead, these products must comply with FDA regulations covering donor screening, tissue processing, safety, and handling, and are processed through FDA-registered tissue establishments.

So let's give credit where it's due. FDA-regulated is the correct term, not FDA-approved.

4. Ready to give fat a chance?

Curious? Here's what to know before taking the next step.

Is Lipoderma my kind of fat?

It might be.

If you've noticed changes in facial fullness from aging, weight loss, or other factors, Lipoderma may be an option worth exploring.

Your provider will consider your goals, medical history, previous aesthetic procedures, and the areas you'd like to restore to help determine whether Lipoderma is right for you.

Can I dissolve Lipoderma if I change my mind?

Not quite. This isn't your typical filler.

Unlike hyaluronic acid fillers, which can often be dissolved using an enzyme called hyaluronidase, Lipoderma is made from human adipose tissue and cannot be dissolved in the same way.

But that's not unusual. Other aesthetic products, like collagen biostimulators, can't simply be dissolved either.

That's why choosing an experienced provider is so important. Proper patient selection, placement, and technique all matter. Be sure to discuss your goals, expectations, and potential options for managing an unwanted result before implantation.

What's the price of a little more fat?

Every face is different. So is every restoration plan.

The cost of Lipoderma depends on the amount needed, the areas being addressed, and your provider's fees. Lipoderma is an elective, self-pay aesthetic procedure.

Your provider can walk you through pricing, the recommended amount, and any potential future touch-ups during your consultation.
