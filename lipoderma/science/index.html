Lipoderma / The science

Real tissue.
Ready when you are.

Almost all fat restoration today is autologous — the patient is their own donor. Britecyte solved the barrier that kept adipose tissue limited to that, which is what makes an off-the-shelf allograft possible.

The pathway

Four steps from
donor to patient.

The technology isn't a formulation. It's a process that removes what the immune system objects to while leaving the tissue's own architecture intact.

01

Qualified donor tissue

Human adipose tissue is recovered from qualified donors under established tissue-banking standards — the biological starting material, with native fat cells and extracellular structure intact before any processing begins.

02

Immunogenicity resolved

Britecyte's proprietary, patent-protected process addresses the immunogenicity that has historically restricted adipose to autologous use.

The tissue is processed to permit allogeneic transplantation while preserving native architecture — not reduced to a synthetic gel or cross-linked filler.

03

A native allograft

The result is a ready-to-use human adipose allograft developed without synthetic agents, cross-linkers, or foreign scaffolds.

Intact adipocytes and extracellular matrix are preserved in a 90:10 ratio — a structure that behaves like the tissue it's replacing, because it is that tissue.

04

Restoration in the patient

Providers place Lipoderma where adipose has been lost, restoring the support beneath healthy-looking skin in the face, hands, and other areas affected by volume loss.

More than 650 patients have received Britecyte allografts to date.
